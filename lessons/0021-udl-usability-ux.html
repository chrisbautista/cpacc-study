UDL, usability and user experience

Win for today: name the three UDL guidelines and their options, and separate usability, user experience and user-centered design. This is Domain Two F. Follows the seven principles of universal design.

1. The idea

Source: BoK Domain Two F, pp. 44–47. Universal Design for Learning (UDL) is a set of guidelines for designing instruction around varied student needs and preferences. It is based on cognitive science and stresses flexibility in presenting and demonstrating knowledge. The guidelines come from CAST (Center for Applied Special Technology).

GuidelineQuestionInstructors give options for
EngagementThe WhyRecruiting interest; sustaining effort; self-regulation
RepresentationThe WhatPerception through different modalities; language and symbols; comprehension through scaffolds
Action & ExpressionThe HowPhysically accessible tools and environments; expressing and communicating; executive functions through scaffolding

Usability vs user experience (UX):

  • Usability: ease of access and use. A sub-discipline of UX, not the whole of it. Three aspects: easy and proficient use; achieving goals easily; easy to learn so return visits are as easy or easier.
  • UX: every interaction with a product, from becoming aware of it to trust and satisfaction. More comprehensive than usability.

Five core areas of UX:

  • Usability
  • Useful content
  • Desirable or pleasurable content
  • Accessibility
  • Credibility (trust: security and privacy)

User-centered design: puts the user at the center of every stage of design and development, so the target audience can use the product and gets a better experience.

  • Involve users from the beginning and throughout, through user research and user testing
  • Iterate: test after each stage, often with users
  • Include accessibility user testing in those iterative cycles

4. Mnemonics (ours, not the BoK's)

  • UDL order: "E-R-A" = Why, What, How. Engagement is the Why (motivation), Representation is the What (the content), Action & Expression is the How (showing it).
  • Each guideline has three options. Engagement: interest, effort, self-regulation. Representation: perception, language, comprehension. Action: tools, expression, executive function.
  • UX areas: "U-U-D-A-C". Usability, Useful content, Desirable content, Accessibility, Credibility.
  • Usability sits inside UX. UX is the whole journey; usability is one part of it.

5. Explain it simply (Feynman technique)

Teachers should give choices because learners differ. Give several ways to get interested (engagement, the why), several ways to take in the content (representation, the what) and several ways to show what they know (action and expression, the how). In products, usability is how easy it is to use. User experience is the whole journey, including content, trust and accessibility. User-centered design involves real users from the start and tests again and again, including with people with disabilities.
