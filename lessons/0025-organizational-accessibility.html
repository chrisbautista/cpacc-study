Integrating accessibility across the organization

Win for today: name the four WAI phases, tell the two maturity models apart, and know what champions, evaluation, hiring and procurement add. This is Domain Three F, the last section of the BoK. Follows domain-specific laws, procurement and ICT standards.

1. The idea

Source: BoK Domain Three F, pp. 64–70. ICT accessibility must be a program, not a project: strategic, integral and ongoing.

Maturity models: a tool for organizations to measure progress and improvement. The BoK gives two examples. The level names look alike but are not the same, and levels 2 and 3 swap.

LevelBusiness Disability Forum (BDF)Capability Maturity Model (CMM, Carnegie Mellon)
1
  • Informal
  • No documentation or process in place
  • Initial
  • Ad hoc and unpredictable
2
  • Defined
  • Documented but not actioned or completed once
  • Repeatable
  • Policies and procedures in place
  • Practiced, documented, enforced, trained, measured, able to improve
3
  • Repeatable
  • Process established and actioned consistently
  • Defined
  • Standard processes documented across the organization and integrated
4
  • Managed
  • Monitored and improved; business as usual
  • Managed
  • Quantitative quality goals; consistent measurements
5
  • Best practice
  • Innovate, improve, and share
  • Optimizing
  • Continuous improvement; prevent defects; best-practice innovations transferred
  • The BDF model is a self-assessment tool based on the Accessible Technology Charter (10 commitments), scored 1 to 5
  • The CMM was designed for software development; version 1.1 is adapted here to ICT accessibility
  • The BoK's printed CMM list is garbled (Repeatable appears under Initial); the levels above are the intended order

Management champions:

  • People from key areas appointed as role models, with deep accessibility understanding
  • They advocate during adoption: engage teams, build awareness and skills, promote accessibility
  • They build a vision, sustain commitment, support programmatic integration (not discrete projects) and lead adoption of a maturity model

Evaluating:

  • Evaluate early and often: cheaper to fix early
  • Consider "born accessible" design and reusable design and code libraries
  • Use evaluation tools, but human evaluation is critical
  • Run formative, summative and continuous evaluations, and include people with disabilities
  • Use outside experts until in-house expertise is built

Recruiting and hiring:

  • People with disabilities: inclusion statement in postings, disability-focused job boards and job fairs, accessible recruitment site, accessible facilities and reasonable accommodations, educate managers
  • Digital accessibility skills: HTML/CSS/JavaScript, testing tools, keyboard and screen reader testing, remediating documents, WCAG 2.1 and PDF/UA
  • Post on the IAAP Career Center, a11yjobs and professional networks

Communications: publish accessibility standards for communications, train communicators in people-first and plain language, make documents and sites accessible, caption and describe video.

Legal and PR: PR consults legal or risk and compliance before publishing accessibility information. Know the laws and standards, assess liability, create and follow an ICT accessibility plan that documents how products meet standards.

Procurement best practices: verify accessibility claims, verify vendor expertise and capacity, require accessibility in contracts, review vendor roadmaps periodically, use procurement policy to influence vendors.

4. Mnemonics (ours, not the BoK's)

  • WAI phases: I-P-I-S. Initiate wins support, Plan writes policy, Implement builds skills, Sustain tracks change.
  • BDF levels: I-D-R-M-B (Informal, Defined, Repeatable, Managed, Best practice).
  • CMM levels: I-R-D-M-O (Initial, Repeatable, Defined, Managed, Optimizing).
  • The swap: BDF writes it down first (Defined), then does it consistently (Repeatable). CMM has Repeatable policies first, then Defined organization-wide standards. Levels 1, 4 and 5 are the same idea in both.
  • Top level: Optimizing and Best practice both mean innovation is shared.
  • European Agency: Policy, Plan, Practice (three Ps).

5. Explain it simply (Feynman technique)

Accessibility in an organization must be a program, not a project. The W3C WAI recommends four phases: Initiate (business case, objectives, support), Plan (policy, budget, responsibilities), Implement (skills, quick wins, early evaluation) and Sustain (monitoring, legislation, user feedback). Maturity models measure progress: the Business Disability Forum model runs Informal, Defined, Repeatable, Managed, Best practice, and Carnegie Mellon's CMM runs Initial, Repeatable, Defined, Managed, Optimizing. Champions from key areas model the behaviour and lead adoption. Hiring, communications, legal and procurement each need accessibility built in.
