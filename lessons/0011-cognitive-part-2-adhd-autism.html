Cognitive disabilities, part 2: ADHD, autism and NLD

Win for today: state the DSM-V criteria and prevalence for ADHD and autism, and tell NLD apart from Asperger syndrome. Follows part 1.

1. The idea

ADHD has two symptom groups: inattention/distraction (short attention span, easily distracted, trouble starting and finishing tasks, forgetfulness) and hyperactive/impulsive (restless, can't stay seated, fidgeting, can't wait a turn, talks too much and interrupts). Not everyone has both: the NHS estimates 2 to 3 in 10 people with ADHD have attention problems without hyperactivity. ADHD can occur together with autism. Most are diagnosed as children; adults can still struggle with work and relationships.

Autism spectrum disorders (ASD) are, per WHO, a group of complex brain development disorders. Asperger syndrome is no longer separate; it is classed as a form of ASD. Autism can occur with or without intellectual disability. DSM-V has three severity levels. NLD (nonverbal learning disability) is very much like Asperger syndrome, with normal intelligence and language development but trouble with social skills, sensory input and transitions. 4. Mnemonics (ours, not the BoK's)

  • Thresholds: kids 6, adults 5. Adults need one fewer symptom.
  • ADHD groups: "I.D.F." vs "F.W.T." Inattention: Distracted, can't Finish. Hyperactive: Fidget, Wait, Talk.
  • Autism has two criteria: S + R. Social communication limits, Repetitive behaviours. Three severity levels.
  • Autism traits: "SCS". Sensory, Communication (non-literal), Social overwhelm.
  • The 1-in-100 twins: autism (1 in 100 people) and NLD (1% of US children) share the same number. Don't swap them with the other conditions.
  • ADHD percentages: children 2 to 7, adults about 4 (roughly the middle). Dyslexia 5 to 10, dyscalculia 3 to 6.
  • NLD vs Asperger: same family. Asperger = more severe.

5. Explain it simply (Feynman technique)

ADHD is when focus and self-control are hard in one or two ways: either you drift off, lose things and can't finish tasks, or you can't sit still, wait your turn or stop talking. Kids need six signs, adults five. Autism is a different way the brain develops. Social back-and-forth and reading unspoken cues are harder, routines and repeated movements matter, and sound, smell and light can feel too strong. It's a spectrum: some people also have an intellectual disability, others live fully independently. About 1 in 100 people are autistic. NLD looks like Asperger syndrome: lots of vocabulary and memory, but trouble with social cues, change and the big picture.
