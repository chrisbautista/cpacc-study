CPACC · Domain Three (20%) · 3C

National and provincial disability laws

Win for today: place the main disability laws by country, year and scope, and tell reasonable accommodation from accessibility. This is Domain Three C. Follows international and regional rights instruments.

1. Explain it simply (Feynman technique)

Since the 1990s, laws have moved from welfare to equality: people with disabilities are rights holders. The US passed Section 508 in 1973 for government technology and the ADA in 1990 as a broad civil rights law. The EU Employment Equality Directive (2000) bans disability discrimination at work and requires reasonable accommodation, unless it is a disproportionate burden. Ontario passed its own provincial act in 2001. The UK Equality Act (2010) covers England, Scotland and Wales and lists disability as one of nine protected characteristics. Reasonable accommodation helps one person at a time; accessibility rules apply whoever the person is.

Explain to your teacher the difference between Section 508 and the ADA. Where you stumble is the next thing to study.

2. The idea

Source: BoK Domain Three C, pp. 55–58.

Why laws changed:

  • Civil rights protections spread quickly since the 1990s. The US adopted the ADA in 1990. In Europe, 33 countries adopted some disability equality protection over 25 years.
  • The move from the medical model to the social model pushed law from welfare and social security toward anti-discrimination and equality law
  • The CRPD was the other main driver
  • Equality laws treat people with disabilities as people with rights, not problems, and challenge segregation and exclusion

Two legal approaches:

ApproachHow it works
Reasonable accommodation (individual accommodation)
  • Equal treatment case by case
  • EU Employment Directive: employers take appropriate measures where needed in a particular case
  • Not required if it imposes a disproportionate burden on the employer
Accessibility
  • Requirements are met regardless of a person's disability status
  • US Section 508 (Rehabilitation Act 1973): when federal agencies develop, procure, maintain or use electronic and information technology, the public with disabilities must get comparable access and use

The laws:

LawYearWhat to know
US Rehabilitation Act, Section 5081973
  • Accessible electronic and information technology
  • Comparable access for members of the public with disabilities
US Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA)1990
  • Civil rights law
  • Protections similar to those for race, color, sex, national origin, age and religion
  • Equal opportunity in public accommodations, employment, transportation, state and local government services, telecommunications
EU Employment Equality Directive2000
  • Prohibits disability discrimination in employment and occupation
  • Requires reasonable accommodation
  • All EU Member States have implemented it
Ontarians with Disabilities Act2001
  • Provincial law (Ontario, Canada)
  • Equal opportunity and freedom from discrimination
  • Goal: no new barriers, remove existing ones
UK Equality Act2010
  • Covers England, Scotland and Wales
  • Brings together and strengthens multiple anti-discrimination laws
  • Disability is one of 9 protected characteristics
  • Protects against direct discrimination, discrimination arising from disability, indirect discrimination

Equality Act protected characteristics (9):

  • Age
  • Disability
  • Gender reassignment
  • Marriage and civil partnership
  • Pregnancy and maternity
  • Race
  • Religion or belief
  • Sex
  • Sexual orientation

EU gaps: a general Anti-discrimination Directive (outside work) was proposed in 2008 but not adopted, so national laws differ. In health care, Member State laws fall into 4 groups: both prohibit discrimination and require reasonable accommodation, prohibit discrimination only, require reasonable accommodation only, or neither.

5. Mnemonics (ours, not the BoK's)

  • Years in order: 73, 90, 00, 01, 10. Section 508 (1973), ADA (1990), EU Employment Directive (2000), Ontario (2001), UK Equality Act (2010).
  • The US has two: Section 508 = technology (1973, the older one); ADA = civil rights, broad (1990).
  • Nine protected characteristics: A-D-G-M-P-R-R-S-S (Age, Disability, Gender reassignment, Marriage, Pregnancy, Race, Religion, Sex, Sexual orientation).
  • Accommodation = one person; accessibility = everyone (the same split as universal design vs accommodation in Lesson 18).
