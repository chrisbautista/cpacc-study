Domain-specific laws, procurement and ICT standards

Win for today: match each domain-specific law, procurement rule and ICT standard to its region and scope, and know who enforces it. This is Domain Three D and E. Follows national and provincial laws.

1. Explain it simply (Feynman technique)

Some laws cover one sector: the US CVAA for modern communications, the Air Carrier Access Act for flights, and in the EU the AVMSD for TV, the EECC for telecoms and eIDAS for electronic signatures. Procurement laws make buyers ask for accessibility: the EU directive 2014/24/EU and the US Federal Acquisition Regulation, which implements Section 508. For ICT, US government sites follow Section 508 while private sites face ADA Title III lawsuits. In the EU, the Web Accessibility Directive covers public sector sites and apps, using the voluntary standard EN 301 549, and the European Accessibility Act covers private businesses from 2025.

Explain to your teacher the difference between the Web Accessibility Directive and the European Accessibility Act. Where you stumble is the next thing to study.

2. The idea

Domain-specific laws target a sector (such as transport) or an activity (such as procurement).

LawRegionWhat to know
CVAA (Twenty-First Century Communications and Video Accessibility Act, 2010)US
  • Overseen by the Federal Communications Commission
  • Updates 1980s and 1990s accessibility laws for digital, broadband and mobile
Air Carrier Access Act (ACAA)US
  • Department of Transportation rule
  • Prohibits disability discrimination in air travel
  • All flights of US airlines, plus foreign airlines' flights to or from the US
  • Air Carrier Access Amendments Act of 2017: aircraft must be designed to accommodate people with disabilities
AVMSD (Audiovisual Media Services Directive, 2010/13/EU)EU
  • Traditional TV and on-demand services
  • Services made continuously and progressively more accessible
  • Emergency information made accessible
  • Encourages accessibility action plans
EECC (European Electronic Communications Code, 2018/1972)EU
  • Telecommunication services
  • Accessible service information and contract summary
  • Accessible missing children and child helpline hotlines
  • Equal access to emergency services
  • Affordable equipment and services for equivalent access
eIDAS RegulationEU
  • Electronic identification and trust services
  • Electronic signatures and related products must be accessible

Procurement laws make sure user needs are accounted for when buying products and services, and detail the accessibility standards they must meet.

LawWhat it does
EU Public Procurement Directive (2014/24/EU)
  • Technical specifications for products used by people must consider accessibility criteria or design for all
  • Existing mandatory EU accessibility requirements must be used
  • Tender evaluation may consider accessibility
  • Operators proven unreliable on accessibility rules may be excluded
  • Electronic procurement documents and communication must take accessibility into account
  • Utilities, transport and concessions directives have similar provisions
  • Member States implement them in national procurement law
US Federal Acquisition Regulation (FAR)
  • Implements Section 508 when agencies acquire ICT
  • Federal employees with disabilities get access comparable to others
  • Members of the public with disabilities get access comparable to others

ICT laws and standards (3E):

RegionWhat to know
US
  • Federal, state and local government websites follow Section 508 Standards, based on WCAG 2.0
  • The ADA has no legal standards for private business sites, yet organizations can be sued
  • ADA Title III covers places of public accommodation: e-commerce, organizational sites, public mobile apps
  • Enforced by the Department of Justice Civil Rights Division; for schools, the Department of Education Office for Civil Rights
  • Enforcement happens through consent decrees and settlements
EU: Web Accessibility Directive (2016/2102)
  • Applied from September 2019
  • Public sector websites and mobile apps
  • Does not require procuring accessible solutions
  • Needs an accessibility statement, a way to report issues and a link to enforcement procedures
EU: EN 301 549
  • Voluntary harmonized European standard
  • Annex A relevant to the Web Accessibility Directive; following it presumes conformance
  • Aligns with WCAG 2.1, extended to downloadable documents and mobile apps
  • Adds biometrics, two-way voice communication, accessibility documentation and support services
  • Other standards may be used, but the body may need to prove compliance
EU: European Accessibility Act (adopted 2019)
  • Common rules apply from 2025
  • Private businesses including manufacturers, representatives, importers and distributors, except microenterprises
  • Covered: computers and operating systems, ATMs, ticketing and check-in machines, smartphones, digital TV equipment, telephony, audiovisual media, passenger transport services, consumer banking, e-books, e-commerce
  • Member States enforce; NGOs and authorities may go to court for a person; penalties allowed

Standards behind the European Accessibility Act (voluntary, harmonized):

  • Revised EN 301 549 (ICT, aligned with the Web Accessibility Directive)
  • Revised EN 17210 (accessibility and usability of the built environment)
  • Revised EN 17161 (Design for All, products, goods and services)
  • New: non-ICT product information
  • New: support services (help desks, call centres, relay services, training)
  • New: emergency communications, including the single European number 112

5. Mnemonics (ours, not the BoK's)

  • Public vs private in the EU: Web Accessibility Directive = public sector (2019); European Accessibility Act = private business (2025). 19 then 25, public then private.
  • Directive vs standard: a directive is law; EN 301 549 is the voluntary standard that helps you meet it.
  • Standard numbers: 301 549 = ICT; 17210 = built environment ("two-one-oh" like a building floor plan); 17161 = Design for All.
  • US two-track: government sites follow Section 508 (WCAG 2.0); private sites have no legal standard but face ADA Title III lawsuits.
  • AVMSD: "AV" is audiovisual, so TV and on-demand video. Not telecoms (EECC) and not signatures (eIDAS).
  • ADA enforcer: a civil rights law is enforced by Justice (DOJ Civil Rights Division). Schools go to Education OCR.
  • EU Charter Article 21 vs 26: 21 = non-discrimination (lists disability); 26 = integration. Our hook: "twenty-one, one rule for everyone".
  • Procurement: EU = 2014/24/EU, US = FAR. Both say "buy accessible".
