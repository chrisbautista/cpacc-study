CPACC · Domain One (40%) · 1A Models of disability

Strengths and weaknesses of all seven models

Win for today: given any strength or weakness, name its model, and the reverse. Closes out section 1A. Builds on Lesson 1 and Lesson 2.

1. Explain it simply (Feynman technique)

Every model is good at something and blind to something else. Medical is good at the body and blind to the world around it. Social is good at the world and can forget the body. Charity inspires giving but talks down to people. Economic recognizes need but stamps a "needy" label on people. Functional solutions gets results but can chase profit. Identity gives pride but can leave outsiders out. Biopsychosocial joins the others but is complicated to use.

Pick any model and explain its strength and weakness to your teacher as if to a friend. Where you hesitate is the next thing to study.

2. The table (short versions)

These are one-line compressions of the BoK, Domain One A, pp. 4–7. The BoK adds detail: for example, the medical model leaves people feeling excluded or pitied, and the social model's activism can create political adversaries.

Mirror pairs help. The social model's strength (barriers aren't inevitable) is exactly what the medical model's weakness misses (ignores environments). The medical model's strength (real biology) is the social model's weakness (downplays the body).

5. Mnemonics (ours, not the BoK's)

  • Medical ⇄ Social are mirrors. One sees the body, the other the barrier; each one's strength is the other's weakness.
  • Charity = "Condescending Cheque". The weakness is the pity; the strength is that people give.
  • Economic = "Earnings Excluded". It recognizes need for support, but the legal "needy" label stigmatizes.
  • Functional = "FIX but check the profit". Practical results; profit-driven products can miss.
  • Identity = "In-group pride, out-group pain".
  • Biopsychosocial = bridge, but a complicated one. Complex classification.
