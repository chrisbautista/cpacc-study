International and regional rights instruments

Win for today: place each treaty, declaration and charter by year, level and scope. This is Domain Three A and B. Follows UDL, usability and UX.

1. Explain it simply (Feynman technique)

The 1948 declaration gave everyone rights but didn't name disability. In 1975 the UN passed a declaration on disabled persons, but declarations only recommend. In 2006 the CRPD made disability rights binding, and treats people as rights holders. In 2013 the Marrakesh Treaty let blind and print-disabled people share accessible books without asking copyright holders. Regions have their own charters: Europe (1950, 1961, 2000), Africa (1981, 2018) and the Americas (1999, the first regional binding disability treaty).

Explain to your teacher what the difference is between a declaration and a convention. Where you stumble is the next thing to study.

2. The idea

Source: BoK Domain Three A and B, pp. 48–54. The BoK says people with disabilities are about 15% of the world, "the world's largest minority" (WHO says 16%; same idea). Legal instruments can do one of three things:

  • Grant fundamental rights to every person, disabled people included
  • Prohibit discrimination, naming disability or covering it under "other status"
  • Address the rights of people with disabilities directly

International (3A):

InstrumentYearWhat to know
Universal Declaration of Human Rights (UDHR)1948
  • Foundation for all human rights conventions
  • Does not list disability
Declaration on the Rights of Disabled Persons1975
  • A declaration, not a convention
  • Gives a framework and recommendations only
Convention on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities (CRPD)2006
  • First binding international instrument on disability rights
  • Sees people as subjects with rights, not objects of charity
  • 160+ signed, 180+ ratified
  • Accessibility: a general principle in Article 3, detailed in Article 9
  • EU signed as a block; the EU and each Member State ratified
Marrakesh Treaty (WIPO)2013
  • Ends the "book famine" for blind, visually impaired and print-disabled people
  • Copyright exception: make and share accessible formats without asking permission
  • Allows import and export of accessible formats

CRPD oversight: states parties must set up a framework to promote, protect and monitor. The Committee on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities reviews implementation and issues a List of Issues; NGOs can submit alternative reports.

Regional (3B):

InstrumentYearWhat to know
European Convention on Human Rights (ECHR)1950
  • Council of Europe treaty
  • Cases go to the European Court of Human Rights
  • Disability is not named; it is covered as "other status"
European Social Charter (ESC)1961
  • Council of Europe treaty
  • Guarantees social and economic rights
  • Includes independence, integration and participation for people with disabilities
African Charter on Human and People's Rights1981
  • Recognizes the rights of all people
  • Disability is not named
  • Has still been used to fight disability discrimination
Inter-American Convention1999
  • Adopted in Guatemala
  • First regional binding treaty expressly prohibiting disability discrimination
  • Accessibility is addressed in Article III
EU Charter of Fundamental Rights2000
  • Adopted in 2000, but at first it was not legally binding
  • Became binding on EU Member States in December 2009, when the Treaty of Lisbon took effect
  • Article 26: integration of persons with disabilities
  • Article 21: non-discrimination, and disability is one of the listed grounds
African Disability Rights Protocol (ADRP)2018
  • Adds detail to the CRPD: armed conflict, forced displacement, harmful practices
  • Not in force as of mid-2023
  • Needs 15 ratifications to come into force

Others listed in the BoK: the ASEAN Human Rights Declaration and the Arab Charter on Human Rights. The CPACC Content Outline lists only three regional instruments.

5. Mnemonics (ours, not the BoK's)

  • International years in order: 1948 UDHR, 1975 Declaration, 2006 CRPD, 2013 Marrakesh. "Rights, Recommend, Require, Read."
  • Regional years in order: 1950 ECHR, 1961 ESC, 1981 Africa, 1999 Americas, 2000 EU Charter, 2018 ADRP. A 1900s run, then 2000 and 2018.
  • Article pairs: CRPD 9 = accessibility; EU Charter 26 = integration, 21 = non-discrimination.
  • Binding vs not: declaration = recommend, convention = require; ADRP = not in force yet.

What "in force" means

A treaty is "in force" when it is legally operating: its rules apply to the countries that have joined it.

  • Adopted: the text is agreed and approved (the African Union did this in 2018)
  • Signed and ratified: each country formally agrees to be bound, through its own government or parliament
  • Enters into force: once enough countries have ratified, a number the treaty itself sets

The African Disability Rights Protocol needs 15 ratifications. As of mid-2023 it had fewer, so it was adopted but not in force: it did not yet legally bind anyone.

Don't mix this up with the EU Charter. It was adopted in 2000 and became binding in December 2009 because the Treaty of Lisbon took effect. Both show a gap between "adopted" and "binding", but the cause is different.

Is "binding" the same as "in force"? Closely related, not the same:

  • Binding: the kind of instrument. Does it create legal obligations? A convention or treaty does; a declaration only recommends. That is why the CRPD is the first binding international disability instrument and the 1975 Declaration is not.
  • In force: its status right now. Is it legally operating yet? A binding treaty only has effect once it enters into force, and then it binds the states that ratified it.
  • Example: the African Disability Rights Protocol is a binding type of instrument, but as of mid-2023 it was not in force (fewer than 15 ratifications), so it bound no one yet.
  • EU Charter: "became legally binding" in 2009 means it took legal effect when the Treaty of Lisbon came into force.
  • For the exam: "legally binding" and "in force" give the same practical result, that the rules apply. Use "binding" for treaty versus declaration, and "in force" for whether the treaty has taken effect.
