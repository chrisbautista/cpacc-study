CPACC · Domain One (40%) · 1B Categories of disability

Psychological disabilities

Win for today: name the three groups of psychological disabilities, tell the anxiety types apart, separate bipolar disorder from psychosis, and hold the new numbers. Follows seizure disabilities.

1. The idea

Psychological disabilities (mental ill-health) affect a person's perceptions, thoughts, feelings, mood and behavior. They can be occasional or long-lasting. Three groups:

  • Anxiety disorders, including panic disorders and phobias
  • Mood disorders, including depression and bipolar disorder
  • Psychotic disorders, including schizophrenia

Source: BoK Domain One B8, pp. 28–31.

ConditionWhat it isNumbers
Anxiety disordersAnxiety that does not go away and can get worse, interfering with work, school, relationships.2.5 to 7% by country; 284 million in 2017, the most prevalent mental health disorder
Generalized anxiety disorder (GAD)Persistent anxiety or dread, for months if not years. Restless, hard to concentrate, tired, poor sleep, aches.
Panic disorderFrequent, unexpected panic attacks: sudden intense fear with no clear trigger. Racing heart, sweating, trembling, chest pain, impending doom.
Social anxiety disorderIntense fear of being watched and judged. Self-conscious, blushing, little eye contact, soft voice.
Bipolar disorderExtreme mood swings (NHS): episodes of depression and episodes of mania, each lasting weeks. Either may include delusions or hallucinations.19 million in 2019 (WHO)
Psychotic disordersAffect thinking and perceptions; people lose touch with reality. Delusions (false beliefs) and hallucinations (perceiving things not there). Schizophrenia is one type.Schizophrenia: 24 million, 1 in 300 (WHO)

Most common mood disorders: depression, bipolar disorder, seasonal affective disorder and self-harm. Psychotic symptoms can also come from stroke, brain tumors, or alcohol or drug addiction.

Barriers:

  • Limited availability and affordability of mental healthcare
  • Providers lacking knowledge for accurate diagnosis and treatment
  • Social stigma
  • Lack of support for cognitive challenges
  • Students: no school support for emotional, behavioural or cognitive challenges

Solutions: many solutions for cognitive disabilities help too, since psychological disabilities affect mental functions. Assistive technology (AT):

  • Apps with mood, stress and anxiety management
  • Memory aids and reminder devices
  • Text-to-speech and voice recognition software
  • Noise monitoring devices

4. Mnemonics (ours, not the BoK's)

  • Three groups: "A-M-P". Anxiety, Mood, Psychotic.
  • Anxiety types: "G-P-S". Generalized (months or years), Panic (sudden, no trigger), Social (watched and judged).
  • Bipolar = two poles: mania (high, overjoyed) and depression (low, hopeless), each lasting weeks.
  • Psychosis: D and H. Delusions are false beliefs; Hallucinations are things you perceive that aren't there.
  • Numbers: anxiety 2.5 to 7% ends at 7, like children's ADHD 2 to 7%. Bipolar 19M is smaller than schizophrenia 24M. Count ladder: 200M < 246M < 284M < 430M < 2.2B.

5. Explain it simply (Feynman technique)

Psychological disabilities change how a person feels, thinks and sees the world. Anxiety is worry that won't switch off: constant (GAD), in sudden waves (panic) or in front of other people (social). Mood disorders swing the feelings: bipolar disorder means weeks of high energy, then weeks of low. Psychosis means losing touch with reality, believing things that aren't true or perceiving things that aren't there. The biggest barriers are cost, stigma and lack of support, and tools like stress apps, reminders and noise monitors help.
