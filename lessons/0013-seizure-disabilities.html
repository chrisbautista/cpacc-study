Seizure disabilities

Win for today: define a seizure and epilepsy, state the photosensitive epilepsy numbers, and match barriers and assistive technology to seizure disorders. Follows part 3.

1. Explain it simply (Feynman technique)

A seizure is a short electrical storm in the brain. If it happens twice or keeps coming back, that's epilepsy. For a small group (about 3 in 100 people with epilepsy), flashing or flickering lights and strong moving patterns can set one off, most often at 16 to 25 flashes a second. So web pages must not flash more than three times in a second, and players must let you stop animations. Help for the person includes alert watches, protective gear and service animals.

2. The idea

A seizure is a sudden, uncontrolled electrical disturbance in the brain. It can change behavior, movements, feelings and consciousness, from mild to severe. Two or more seizures, or a tendency to recurrent seizures, is epilepsy. Symptoms range from visual hallucinations and inability to speak to convulsions and falling. WHO: 50 million people have epilepsy, one of the most common neurological diseases.

Photosensitive epilepsy means seizures triggered by flashing or flickering lights or patterns. There are two groups of people with photosensitive epilepsy: those whose seizures come only from flashing or flickering lights or patterns (pure photosensitivity), and those who have seizures from those triggers and also have seizures with no light trigger at other times. About 3% of people with epilepsy have it. Source: BoK Domain One B7, p. 27.

PatternsHigh-contrast or moving patterns are more likely triggers. Some video games contain provocative light stimulation.
Web limitNo more than 3 flashes in any one second, or below the general and red flash thresholds.

Barriers:

  • Activities where sudden loss of consciousness could cause serious harm (swimming, taking a bath, power tools)
  • Moving, blinking or flickering content in videos, films, websites and other technologies
  • Browsers and media players without controls to stop or turn off video or animations

Assistive technology:

  • Mobile digital diary apps with reminders
  • Smart watches that detect seizures and send alerts or GPS location
  • Service animals
  • Wearables with an alert button to call for help
  • Supportive gear and protective wear in case of a fall
  • Flicker-free monitors, monitor glare guards, non-glare glasses

5. Mnemonics (ours, not the BoK's)

  • Two seizures = epilepsy. One seizure alone is not epilepsy.
  • The three 3s: 3 flashes per second is the web limit; about 3% of people with epilepsy are photosensitive; 3 per second is the lowest sensitive rate.
  • "16 to 25" = sweet spot of the trigger. Extremes 3 and 60.
  • 50 million with epilepsy. Don't swap it with the other counts: 200M < 246M < 430M < 2.2B.
  • Barriers: "BFC". Bath-type dangers, Flicker content, Controls missing.
