CPACC · Domain One (40%) · 1B Categories of disability

Visual disabilities

Win for today: define blindness, low vision and color vision deficiency, and recall their key numbers. First lesson on section 1B, building on the models lesson.

1. The three types

Visual disabilities are sensory disabilities: some vision loss, loss of visual acuity (sharpness), changed sensitivity to colors, or complete or uncorrectable loss in one or both eyes. Source: BoK Domain One B, pp. 10–11 (figures from the WHO, the World Health Organization, and the US National Institutes of Health).

TypeDefinitionKey numbers
BlindnessSome, near-complete or complete vision loss. May see light/dark or large shapes but can't read text or recognize faces.2.2 billion with vision impairment or blindness (WHO); ≥1 billion preventable or unaddressed. Leading causes: uncorrected refractive errors, cataracts. Most are over 50.
Color vision deficiencyCan't distinguish certain color combinations; reds and greens most common.Red-green: 1 in 12 males (8.3%), 1 in 200 females (0.5%). Blue-yellow: fewer than 1 in 10,000, sexes equal.
Low visionFunctional: uncorrectable loss that interferes with daily activities. Not fixed by glasses, lenses, medicine or surgery.246 million (3.5%). About 90% of people with vision impairment live in low-income settings.

2. Three assistive technologies to know now

  • Screen reader: converts text and structural information to speech.
  • Audio description: an extra audio track describing essential visual information.
  • Screen magnification: for people with low vision. Related design rules: allow magnification, use high contrast, and never rely on color alone.

5. Mnemonics (ours, not the BoK's)

  • 2.2 billion, 1 billion fixable. "Two-two, one preventable."
  • Red-green: a dozen men, 200 women. 1 in 12 vs 1 in 200 (8.3% vs 0.5%).
  • Blue-yellow is rare and fair: under 1 in 10,000, equal sexes.
  • Low vision is "low" but not "fixable": 246 million, 3.5%. Judge by function.
  • Screen reader reads; audio description describes. Reads the interface; describes the picture.
  • Over 50, low income (90%). Who is most affected.

Explain it simply (Feynman technique)

Blindness means seeing little or nothing. Low vision means your sight is reduced in a way glasses can't fix, so it's defined by what you can't do rather than by an eye-chart number. Color vision deficiency means some colors blur together, mostly red and green, and it hits about 1 in 12 men but 1 in 200 women. Helpers: a screen reader reads the screen out loud, and audio description narrates what's happening in a video. When you design, never use color as the only way to say something. Roughly 2.2 billion people live with some vision impairment, and about half of those cases could have been prevented or treated.
