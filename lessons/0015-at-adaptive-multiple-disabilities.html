CPACC · Domain One (40%) · 1B Categories of disability

Assistive technology, adaptive strategies and multiple disabilities

Win for today: define assistive technology, adaptive strategies and accessibility the way the BoK does, and describe multiple or complex disabilities. This closes 1B and 1C. Follows psychological disabilities.

1. The idea

The BoK says 1B (categories) and 1C (assistive technology and adaptive strategies) are grouped together under each disability, which is how lessons 4 to 14 covered them. Three definitions open the section (BoK pp. 9–10):

TermMeaningExample
Assistive technology (AT)Products, devices, systems or items people with disabilities use to perform tasks they could not do otherwise. Called adaptive technologies or adaptive software with computers.Cardboard communication board; screen reader
Adaptive strategiesTweaks and adjustments people use for daily living, to increase independence and participation. They can involve software settings.Moving closer to a speaker in an auditorium
AccessibilityDesigning products, services and environments for equal access for everybody, including people with disabilities. The provider, producer or owner is responsible.Building access, technology, transport, other services
  • Not all AT is computer-based. Cardboard communication boards are AT.
  • Some AT relies on the output of user agents: browsers (graphical, text, voice), multimedia players, plug-ins.
  • In some contexts, AT means only items provided through a social security system (narrow).
  • In other contexts, AT means any product, tool or system available to anyone, free or for purchase (wide).
  • Solutions and AT are relevant to ICT or the physical world.

Multiple or complex disabilities means more than one disability at the same time: physical, mental or both. In education the category is for students with the most profound disabilities. In some countries, like the US, it excludes deaf-blindness, which has its own classification in legislation. Children typically show deficits in five areas:

  • Intellectual functioning
  • Adaptive skills
  • Motor skills
  • Sensory functioning
  • Communication skills

No global statistics exist. UK and Australian sources say 10 to 15% of people with disabilities have complex needs. Solutions follow whichever physical or cognitive needs apply. AT: programmable keyboards, writing support tools, communication aids, text-to-braille software, other adaptive equipment. Source: BoK Domain One B9, pp. 31–32.

4. Mnemonics (ours, not the BoK's)

  • "Thing, Tweak, Design." AT is a Thing (an item), adaptive strategy is a Tweak (an adjustment), accessibility is Design (and the provider owns it).
  • Five deficit areas: "I-A-M-S-C". Intellectual, Adaptive, Motor, Sensory, Communication.
  • Deaf-blindness is the odd one out in the US definition: it has its own legal classification, so it is not "multiple".
  • 10 to 15% have complex needs: a small slice, "one in seven or so".

5. Explain it simply (Feynman technique)

Assistive technology is a thing that lets someone do what they couldn't, like a cardboard picture board or a screen reader. An adaptive strategy is a tweak, like sitting closer or changing a setting. Accessibility is designing so everyone can use something, and whoever provides it owns that job. Some people have several disabilities at once, which is called multiple or complex, and the solution is whichever physical or cognitive supports match their needs.
