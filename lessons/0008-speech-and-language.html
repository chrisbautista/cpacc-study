Speech and language disabilities

Win for today: separate speech from language, name apraxia, dysarthria, mutism types and aphasia, and recall their numbers. Follows Deaf-Blindness.

1. The idea

Speech and language conditions are related and often grouped together, but they differ. Language is understanding, formulating and sharing ideas through words. Speech is the physical way people say words and make sounds. Someone may be unable to speak yet read, write and understand perfectly. Source: BoK Domain One B, pp. 15–17.

2. Tools and solutions to know now

  • Producing speech: text-to-speech (TTS) and Augmentative and Alternative Communication (AAC) devices.
  • Understanding language: screen readers with adjustable rate, voice and pitch.
  • Solutions: extra time, patience, text-based alternatives to speech, multiple communication options.

5. Mnemonics (ours, not the BoK's)

  • Apraxia = A-plan fails. Dysarthria = "dys-muscle": can't control the muscles.
  • Aphasia = A-phase: language lost. Brain injury, usually stroke. Affects reading and writing too.
  • Elective chooses; Selective is situational; Total is silence.
  • Neuro = brain injury; Psycho = mind.
  • Numbers: selective mutism under 1% (0.47–0.76); child speech disorders 5–25, adults 1–2; aphasia 2 million USA, 250,000 GB.

6. Explain it simply (Feynman technique)

Speech is the sound your mouth makes. Language is the ideas inside your head and the words that carry them. Someone can have perfect ideas and still struggle to say them. In apraxia, the brain knows the plan but can't send the movement instructions. In dysarthria, the muscles themselves are hard to control. Aphasia is different: a brain injury, usually a stroke, damages language itself, so words get lost in speaking, reading and writing. Some people don't speak at all: because of brain injury (neurogenic), or the mind: choosing silence (elective), freezing from anxiety (selective), or total silence. Helpers are speech devices (AAC and text-to-speech), extra time and patience, and a way to type instead of talk.
