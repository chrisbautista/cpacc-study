CPACC · Domain One (40%) · 1B Categories of disability

Visual numbers, retested

Win for today: recall the visual-disability statistics from memory. Aim for about 15/18 before we move to auditory. Retest of Lesson 4.

1. Explain it simply (Feynman technique)

Think of the world's population as a crowd. 2.2 billion people have some vision problem, and about half of them could have been helped. A much smaller group, about 3.5 in every 100 people, has low vision: sight that glasses can't fix. Color blindness is mostly a boys' thing: roughly 1 man in 12 but only 1 woman in 200.

Explain the numbers back to your teacher in your own words. Where you stumble is the next thing to study.

2. Write first, then check

Close your eyes or grab paper and write from memory: the number for impairment or blindness, for low vision (count and percent), and the red-green rates for males and females. Then compare. Effort before the answer is what builds memory (see the cheat sheet).

3. Practice: retrieve it

4. Mnemonics (ours, not the BoK's)

  • 2.2 → 1: of 2.2 billion, at least 1 billion preventable.
  • 246 million is 3.5%: "2-4-6 counting up, three and a half in every hundred."
  • Men 12, women 200: a dozen men, two hundred women (8.3% vs 0.5%).
  • Screen reader reads, audio description describes.
  • Low vision = low function: judged by what you can do, not an eye chart.
