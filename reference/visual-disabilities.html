Visual disabilities

Source: BoK pp. 10–12. Index · Acronyms

Numbers to memorize: 2.2 billion (impairment or blindness) · ≥1 billion preventable/unaddressed · 246 million = 3.5% (low vision) · red-green: 1 in 12 males (8.3%), 1 in 200 females (0.5%) · blue-yellow: <1 in 10,000 · ~90% in low-income settings.

Big-three counts: 2.2 billion = vision impairment or blindness · 246 million = low vision · 430 million = disabling hearing loss (see auditory).

TypeDefinitionKey numbers
BlindnessSome, near-complete or complete vision loss. May see light/dark or large shapes but can't read text or recognize faces.2.2 billion with vision impairment or blindness (WHO); ≥1 billion preventable or unaddressed. Leading causes: uncorrected refractive errors, cataracts. Most are over 50.
Color vision deficiencyCan't distinguish certain color combinations; reds and greens most common.Red-green: 1 in 12 males (8.3%), 1 in 200 females (0.5%). Blue-yellow: fewer than 1 in 10,000, sexes equal.
Low visionFunctional: uncorrectable loss that interferes with daily activities. Not fixed by glasses, lenses, medicine or surgery.246 million (3.5%). About 90% of people with vision impairment live in low-income settings.

Assistive tech: screen reader (text and structure to speech), audio description (extra audio track), screen magnification. Rule: never rely on color alone.
