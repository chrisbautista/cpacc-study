Psychological disabilities

Source: BoK pp. 28–31. Lesson: 14. Map.

ConditionKey factsNumbers
Anxiety disordersAnxiety that persists and worsens. Types: GAD, panic, social anxiety, phobias.2.5 to 7% by country; 284 million (2017), most prevalent
GADPersistent anxiety or dread for months or years. Restless, poor concentration, tired, poor sleep, aches.
Panic disorderFrequent unexpected panic attacks, no clear trigger. Racing heart, sweating, trembling, chest pain, impending doom.
Social anxietyIntense fear of being watched and judged. Self-conscious, blushing, little eye contact, soft voice.
Mood disordersDepression, bipolar disorder, seasonal affective disorder, self-harm.
Bipolar disorderExtreme mood swings: depression (low energy, hopeless) and mania (high energy, overjoyed, risky choices), each lasting weeks.19 million (2019, WHO)
Psychotic disordersLose touch with reality. Delusions (false beliefs), hallucinations (perceiving what is not there). Schizophrenia is one type.Schizophrenia 24 million, 1 in 300 (WHO)

Bipolar hook: two poles. Mania is up (overjoyed, big plans, risky choices); depression is down (sad, hopeless, low energy). 19M bipolar is smaller than schizophrenia 24M.

Count ladder: 200M (intellectual disability) < 246M (low vision) < 284M (anxiety) < 430M (hearing loss) < 2.2B (any vision impairment).

Barriers

  • Limited availability and affordability of mental healthcare
  • Providers lack knowledge for diagnosis and treatment
  • Social stigma
  • Lack of support for cognitive challenges
  • Students lack school support for emotional, behavioural, cognitive challenges

Assistive technology

  • Mood, stress and anxiety management apps
  • Memory aids, reminder devices
  • Text-to-speech, voice recognition
  • Noise monitoring devices

Also use solutions for cognitive disabilities.
