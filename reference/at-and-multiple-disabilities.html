AT, adaptive strategies, multiple disabilities

Source: BoK pp. 9–10, 31–32. Lesson: 15. Map.

TermMeaning
Assistive technology (AT)Products, devices, systems or items used by people with disabilities to do tasks they could not do otherwise. Adaptive technology or adaptive software with computers. Not all computer-based (cardboard communication board). Some rely on user agents (browsers, media players, plug-ins). In some contexts, only items provided through a social security system (narrow); in others, anything available to anyone, free or for purchase (wide).
Adaptive strategiesTweaks and adjustments for daily living that increase independence. May involve software settings. Example: move closer to a speaker in an auditorium.
AccessibilityDesigning products, services, environments for equal access for everybody including people with disabilities. Applies to the physical environment, technology, transport, other services. Provider, producer or owner is responsible.
Multiple or complex disabilitiesMore than one disability at once (physical, mental or both). Education: students with the most profound disabilities. Some countries (US) exclude deaf-blindness (own legal classification). 10 to 15% of people with disabilities have complex needs (UK, Australia); no global statistics.

Deficit areas in children with multiple disabilities (I-A-M-S-C)

  • Intellectual functioning
  • Adaptive skills
  • Motor skills
  • Sensory functioning
  • Communication skills

AT for multiple or complex disabilities

  • Programmable keyboards
  • Writing support tools
  • Communication aids
  • Text-to-braille translation software
  • Other adaptive equipment
