The seven principles of universal design

Source: BoK pp. 42–43. Lesson: 20. Index · Acronyms · Map

1997, NCSU working group led by Ronald Mace. "Universal design is good design."

PrincipleKey ideas
1. Equitable UseSame means for all, no stigma, equal privacy and safety, appealing
2. Flexibility in UseChoice, left or right hand, accuracy, own pace
3. Simple and Intuitive UseNo complexity, expectations, literacy range, importance order, prompting and feedback
4. Perceptible InformationSeveral modes, contrast, legibility, describable, device-compatible
5. Tolerance for ErrorHazards isolated, warnings, fail-safe, discourage unconscious action
6. Low Physical EffortNeutral position, reasonable force, less repetition, less sustained effort
7. Size and Space for Approach and UseLine of sight, reach, grip sizes, space for assistive devices

Hook: E-F-S-P-T-L-S. Not the four WCAG principles (POUR).
