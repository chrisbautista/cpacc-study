Speech and language disabilities

Source: BoK pp. 15–17. Index · Acronyms

TermMeaningNumbers
Speech vs languageSpeech = how words and sounds are made. Language = understanding, formulating and sharing ideas through words (also reading, writing).Child prevalence of speech sound disorders 5–25%; adults 1–2%
Apraxia of speechKnows what to say; brain struggles to plan the movements.—
DysarthriaDifficulty controlling the muscles used to speak.—
MutismNeurogenic = brain injury. Psychogenic = elective (chooses), selective (anxiety, situational), total (none).Selective: 0.47% to 0.76%
AphasiaLanguage impairment from brain injury (stroke most common); affects speech, comprehension, reading, writing.≥2,000,000 USA; ≥250,000 Great Britain

Barriers: complex communication systems, not enough time, impatience, no alternatives to speech. Tools: TTS, AAC devices, speech-generating keyboards, communication boards, voice carryover, word prediction, speech-to-text, screen readers.
