# CPACC Exam Overview

> Source: IAAP CPACC Body of Knowledge v4.0 (Oct 2023 content, ©2025), pp. 1–3, and the IAAP
> Professional Certification Handbook (Jan 2026 ed. #2).

## What the credential is

CPACC is IAAP's **foundational** certification — the baseline credential for both technical and
non-technical accessibility roles. It tests **broad, cross-disciplinary conceptual knowledge**, not
hands-on coding. Three knowledge areas:

1. Disabilities
2. Accessibility and universal design
3. Accessibility-related standards, laws, and management strategies

Relevant domains it applies to: web/ICT, architecture and the built environment, consumer and
industrial design, transportation systems, and any domain where design, policy, and management can
improve disability access.

**Related credentials:** WAS (Web Accessibility Specialist) and ADS (Accessible Document Specialist)
are the technical certs. **CPACC + WAS = CPWA** (Certified Professional in Web Accessibility), a
higher-level credential. Know these relationships — they show up as questions.

## Domain weightings — memorize these

| Domain | Topic | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| **One** | Disabilities, Challenges, and Assistive Technologies | **40%** |
| **Two** | Accessibility and Universal Design | **40%** |
| **Three** | Standards, Laws, and Management Strategies | **20%** |

### Domain One at a glance
- Theoretical models of disability
- Categories of disabilities and associated barriers (ICT and physical world)
- Assistive technologies and adaptive strategies
- Disability demographics and statistics
- Disability etiquette

### Domain Two at a glance
- Individual accommodations versus universal design
- Benefits of accessibility
- Accessibility in ICT (WCAG 2.1)
- Accessibility in the physical world (Principles of Universal Design 2.0)
- Universal Design for Learning (UDL) guidelines
- Accessibility, usability, and user experience (UX)

### Domain Three at a glance
- International conventions and treaties on disability rights
- Categories of disability laws and regulations
- Applying accessibility standards and regulations to ICT
- Organizational governance and management

## Scoring

- IAAP does **not** issue percentage scores. You get a **scaled score from 200 to 800**.
- **Passing mark: 600.**
- Result is pass/fail against that scale; you also get domain-level feedback.

## Retake policy

- If you fail, you must **wait two exam sessions** before reapplying.
- You may retake at a **reduced rate once** within one year of the first attempt.
- Maximum **two retakes within a year** of the original exam.

## Format and proctoring

Delivered through **Pearson VUE** — either at a test center or via **remote (online) proctoring**.

If testing remotely:
- Run the **Pearson system check** twice: well before exam day, and again **one hour before** your exam.
- Confirm your device and physical space meet Pearson's testing requirements (quiet, private, clear desk).
- Remote proctoring is **not available** in China, Cuba, Iran, North Korea, Russia, Sudan, or Syria.

> **Verify before you register.** Question count, duration, fees, and session windows change. Check
> the current values on IAAP's site (About CPACC → Prepare for the CPACC Exam, and the Certification
> Exam Session Dates page) rather than trusting any secondhand number, including this file.

## Eligibility

There are **no prerequisites** — no degree requirement, no work-experience requirement. Anyone can sit
for CPACC. Discounts exist for IAAP members, students, and some group/organizational arrangements;
check the Exam Discounts and Payment Options page.

## What the BoK explicitly says about itself

- It is a **starting point**, not an exhaustive explanation of every exam concept.
- It does **not guarantee** exam success, and neither does any prep course.
- The **Additional Reading** section (pp. 71–73) exists for deeper study — treat it as optional given
  a compressed timeline.
