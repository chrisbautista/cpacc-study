## 1. The seven models of disability — one-line tells

| # | Model | One-line tell |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Medical** | The problem is **in the person**; cure or manage it |
| 2 | **Social** | The problem is **in society**; remove the barriers |
| 3 | **Biopsychosocial** | **Bridges medical + social**. Engel 1977 → WHO ICF 2002 |
| 4 | **Economic** | Disability = **inability to work**; benefits, productivity, cost |
| 5 | **Functional Solutions** | **Engineer around** the limitation; tech and innovation |
| 6 | **Social Identity / Cultural Affiliation** | **Pride and community**; Deaf culture |
| 7 | **Charity** | People are **unfortunate and need help**; pity and donations |

**Linkages:** Charity ↔ medical **and** economic. Biopsychosocial = medical + social. ICF derives from
biopsychosocial. Functional solutions = the accessibility professional's lens. Medical → social shift
drives welfare law → anti-discrimination law.

## 2. The 7 Principles of Universal Design

**1997 · Ronald Mace · North Carolina State University (NCSU)**

1. **Equitable Use** — same means for all; don't segregate or stigmatize
2. **Flexibility in Use** — choice of methods; left/right handed; user's pace
3. **Simple and Intuitive Use** — eliminate complexity; match expectations; any literacy level
4. **Perceptible Information** — redundant modes (pictorial, verbal, tactile); contrast; legibility
5. **Tolerance for Error** — minimize hazards; warnings; fail safe; discourage unconscious action
6. **Low Physical Effort** — neutral body position; reasonable forces; minimize repetition
7. **Size and Space for Approach and Use** — line of sight; reach; hand and grip size; room for AT

*Arc:* **who** can use it → **how** → understanding → perceiving → mistakes → effort → room.

## 3. WCAG — POUR (4 principles, 13 guidelines)

| Principle | # of guidelines | The gist |
|---|---|---|
| **Perceivable** | 4 | Text alternatives · captions/multimedia alternatives · presentable different ways without losing meaning · easier to see and hear |
| **Operable** | 5 | Keyboard · enough time · no seizures · navigate and find · inputs beyond keyboard |
| **Understandable** | 3 | Readable · predictable · avoid and correct mistakes |
| **Robust** | 1 | Maximize compatibility with current and future user tools |

**4 + 5 + 3 + 1 = 13.**

**Also benefits (permanent / temporary / situational):** small-screen devices · older people with
changing abilities · temporary disabilities (broken arm, lost glasses) · situational limits (bright
sunlight, can't use audio) · slow or expensive connections.

## 5. Accessibility vs. Usability vs. Universal Design

- **Accessibility** → **people with disabilities**, equivalent experience, no barriers or discrimination
- **Usability** → **ease of use**; does **not always** consider disability
- **Universal design** → **everyone**, **no target group specified**

**Related terms (same idea):** inclusive design · design for all (**European** term; **EN 17161:2019**) ·
human-centered design · life-span design

**Universal design vs. accommodation:** UD is proactive and for as many people as possible without
adaptation; reasonable accommodation is a specific, case-by-case modification. **UD doesn't prevent
accommodation — it reduces the need for it.**

## 8. Statistics — the numbers most likely to be tested

**Global**
- **1.3 billion** people with significant disability = **16%** of world population = **1 in 6**
- Die **up to 20 years** earlier · **2×** the risk of depression, asthma, diabetes, stroke, obesity, poor oral health
- Find transportation **15×** more difficult
- People with disabilities = **~15%**, the "**world's largest minority**" (older framing, still in the BoK)

**Vision**
- **2.2 billion** with vision impairment or blindness; **≥1 billion** preventable/unaddressed
- **Low vision: 246 million = 3.5%**; **~90%** live in low-income settings
- **Red-green CVD: 1 in 12 males (8.3%), 1 in 200 females (0.5%)**
- **Blue-yellow CVD: <1 in 10,000**, sexes equally
- Leading causes: **uncorrected refractive errors and cataracts**; most affected are **over 50**

**Hearing**
- **430 million** with disabling hearing loss
- **750,000** in the EU use sign language as a first language
- **Central Auditory Processing Disorder: ~5%** globally

**Deaf-Blindness: 0.2%–2%** of world population

**Speech and language**
- Speech sound disorders: **children 5–25%**, **adults 1–2%**
- Selective mutism: **0.47%–0.76%**
- Aphasia: **≥2,000,000 USA**, **≥250,000 Great Britain**

**Mobility: 11% of US adults** have mobility disabilities

**Cognitive**
- Intellectual disability: **~200 million** worldwide; **IQ below 70–75**
- Dyslexia: **5–10%** (up to **17%**); **70–80%** of people with reading difficulties
- Dyscalculia: **3–6%**
- ADHD: children **2–7%**, adults **~4%**; **2–3 in 10** have attention issues without hyperactivity
- Autism: **~1 in 100**
- NLD: **~1 in 100 (1%)** of US children

**Seizure**
- Epilepsy: **50 million** globally
- Photosensitive epilepsy: **~3% of people with epilepsy**
- Trigger range: **16–25 flashes/second** most likely; sensitivity as low as **3**, as high as **60**
- **Rule: nothing flashes more than 3 times in any 1 second**

**Psychological**
- Anxiety disorders: **2.5–7%** by country; **284 million (2017)** — **most prevalent** mental health disorder
- Bipolar: **19 million (2019)**
- Schizophrenia: **24 million = 1 in 300**

**Multiple/complex: 10–15%** of people with disabilities have complex needs

**CRPD: 160+ signed, 180+ ratified. ADRP: needs 15 ratifications, not yet in force (mid-2023).**

## 10. The two maturity models — the swap trap

| Level | **Business Disability Forum** | **Capability Maturity Model (CMU)** |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Informal** — no documentation or process | **Initial** — ad hoc, unpredictable |
| 2 | **Defined** — documented, not actioned / once | **Repeatable** — policies in place |
| 3 | **Repeatable** — established, actioned consistently | **Defined** — standard processes documented and integrated |
| 4 | **Managed** — monitored, improved; business as usual | **Managed** — quantitative quality goals, consistent measurement |
| 5 | **Best practice** — innovate, improve, share | **Optimizing** — continuous improvement, prevent defects |

⚠️ **Levels 2 and 3 swap names between the two models.** BDF is based on the **Accessible Technology
Charter** and its **ten commitments**.

**CMM level 2 "Repeatable" processes are:** practiced · documented · enforced · trained · measured ·
able to improve.

## 11. Organizational frameworks

**W3C WAI four phases: Initiate → Plan → Implement → Sustain**
- *Initiate:* learn basics, explore environment, set objectives, business case, raise awareness, gather support
- *Plan:* accessibility policy, assign responsibilities, budget, review environment/websites, monitoring framework, engage stakeholders
- *Implement:* build skills, integrate into policies, assign tasks, **evaluate early and regularly**, prioritize issues, track progress
- *Sustain:* monitor, engage, **track standards and legislation**, adapt to new tech, incorporate user feedback

**European Agency model: Policy → Plan → Practice**

**Accessibility "must be a program, not a project."**

## 13. Disability etiquette — the five rules + language

1. Speak to the person **directly**
2. Don't make **assumptions** about ability
3. Offer help **only if requested** or after asking permission
4. Acknowledge their ability to **make their own decisions**
5. Respect **personal space and equipment** — don't touch a wheelchair without permission

**Person-first** ("person with a disability") — used by the **UN CRPD** and **G3ict/IAAP**. Emphasis on
the person; avoids labelling and stigmatization.
**Identity-first** ("disabled person") — often preferred by **self-advocates**; disability as key identity.
**The resolving rule: ask the person what they prefer.**

## 14. Three limitations of disability statistics

1. **Census surveys usually don't include details** about different disabilities
2. **Terminology varies between countries** — hard to compare
3. **Many people have more than one disability** and fall between defined groups

## 15. Fast definitions that get confused

- **Assistive technology** = a *thing you use* that lets you do what you otherwise couldn't. Not always computer-based.
- **Adaptive strategy** = a *thing you do* — a tweak or adjustment you make yourself.
- **Accessibility** = the *provider's responsibility* to design for equal access.
- **Audio description** = extra audio track describing essential **visual** information.
- **CART / STTR** = live captioning.
- **AAC** = Augmentative and Alternative Communication.
- **Apraxia** = knows what to say, brain can't plan the movements. **Dysarthria** = can't control the muscles.
- **Aphasia** = language disorder from brain injury; affects **all** language use, most commonly from **stroke**.
- **Dyslexia** = reading. **Dyscalculia** = arithmetic. **Dysgraphia** = drawing/copying figures and graphs.
- **APD** = can hear fine, **can't interpret/organize/analyze** what's heard.
- **Ataxia** = loss of fine motor skills from neurological damage.
- **Epilepsy** = **two or more** seizures, or a tendency to recurrent seizures.
- **Delusions** = believing untrue things. **Hallucinations** = perceiving things that aren't there.
- **Declaration** = framework and recommendations. **Convention** = binding.
- **Harmonized standard** = voluntary, but following it **presumes conformance** with the law.
